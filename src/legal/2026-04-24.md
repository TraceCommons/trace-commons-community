This is the whole of it: what you agree to, what happens to your data, what each
consent scope permits, and how to take it back.

Each section says the plain thing first, in ordinary words. The exact clause
follows it, indented. Where the two differ the clause is what governs — so if a
plain-language summary here is wrong, that is a fault to be fixed in this
document, not a licence to read the clause loosely.

Every trace submission records the policy version it was made under. These terms
apply to a trace as they read on the day it was submitted.

## B. Data policy

### B.1 What a submission actually contains

A redacted envelope, and nothing else. It carries the conversation events of one
agent session, metadata about the engine and model that produced it, counts of
what redaction removed, and hashes. It does not carry your raw session file,
your filesystem, or your credentials. Nothing is sent until you have seen the
preview and approved it.

> **Clause B.1.** Each submission is a structured envelope comprising: session
> and event records for a single agent session; engine, channel and model
> metadata; consent metadata (the policy version, the granted scopes, and flags
> recording whether message text and tool payloads are included); privacy
> metadata (redaction pipeline version, per-category redaction counts,
> residual-risk label, redaction hash, and any warnings); and a pseudonymous
> contributor reference with a revocation handle. The consent flags are
> descriptive: they record what the payload contains, and are not a setting that
> changes it.

### B.2 Redaction happens on your machine first

Discovery, parsing and secret scrubbing all run locally. Raw content is never
sent somewhere else to be cleaned — the cleaning happens before anything leaves,
and `submit --dry-run` shows you exactly what would go.

> **Clause B.2.** The contributor client discovers local agent session files,
> parses them, and applies deterministic redaction locally. Only the redacted
> envelope is transmitted. The server re-applies the same deterministic
> redaction on receipt; a submission whose server-side redaction disagrees with
> the client's is treated as a failure rather than accepted.

### B.3 What happens after it arrives

Three things: the text goes through a second, model-based privacy filter; the
trace is scored for novelty and complexity to decide whether it enters the
corpus; and the result is encrypted and stored. The privacy filter is mandatory
on this deployment — if it cannot run, the submission is refused rather than
accepted unfiltered.

> **Clause B.3.** On receipt the server (i) re-applies deterministic redaction;
> (ii) submits message text to a model-based privacy filter, which is required
> by configuration on this deployment, so a filter outage fails the submission
> closed; (iii) scores the trace against novelty and perplexity floors to
> determine corpus admission; and (iv) stores the resulting artifact encrypted
> under a per-artifact data key, itself wrapped by a key held in Google Cloud
> KMS. Submissions carrying a residual-PII risk label above the deployment's
> configured tolerance are quarantined rather than admitted. This deployment
> currently accepts submissions labelled medium risk.

### B.4 Who else touches it

Two companies, for two jobs:

- **Google Cloud** — hosting, the database, encrypted object storage and the key
  management service, in the US-Central region.
- **NEAR AI** — the privacy filter and the scoring model both run there, on
  hardware-isolated inference. Message text is sent to it, in chunks, as part of
  processing every submission.

That second one is worth reading twice: content you submit is sent to a third
party as a normal part of processing it. It is redacted before it goes, and it
goes to a trusted-execution environment rather than an ordinary API, but it does
leave our infrastructure.

> **Clause B.4.** We use the following sub-processors: Google LLC (compute,
> managed PostgreSQL, object storage and key management, region us-central1);
> and NEAR AI (TEE-hosted large-language-model inference, used for the prose-PII
> privacy filter and for perplexity scoring). Redacted message text is
> transmitted to the latter as part of processing each submission. We will
> publish changes to this list by amending this document.

### B.5 Our logs do not contain your content

Audit rows and error logs record hashes and labels, never trace bodies, URLs,
tokens or contributor identity. This is enforced in the code, not merely
intended.

> **Clause B.5.** Operational logging and audit records are hash-only or
> label-only by design. Raw trace content, credentials, and identifying
> references are excluded from stored audit rows and log strings.

### B.6 How long we keep it

Until you withdraw it. There is no automatic expiry on this deployment today — a
trace you submit and never withdraw is kept. If we introduce a retention
schedule, it will appear here first.

> **Clause B.6.** Submitted artifacts are retained until withdrawn. No automated
> retention or pruning schedule is configured on this deployment. Deleted
> objects remain recoverable from the storage provider's soft-delete buffer for
> seven days after deletion, after which they are purged.

### B.7 What the public figures do and do not protect

Published aggregates suppress any cell that would describe fewer than two
contributors. That is suppression, not statistical noise: it stops the most
obvious re-identification, and it is not a guarantee against a determined
analyst combining several published figures. A calibrated noise mechanism is
intended as the contributor base grows; it does not exist yet, and this
deployment publishes on a suppression-only basis and says so in the snapshot it
publishes.

> **Clause B.7.** Community aggregates are published under a minimum-cell
> threshold of two and a declared publication basis of suppression only. No
> differential privacy or noise-addition mechanism is currently applied.
> Leaderboard rows are published only for contributors who have granted
> `public_attribution`.

## C. Consent scopes

You pick these at enrolment, and they travel inside every envelope you submit.
They are cumulative in what they permit, except the last, which is about your
name rather than your traces. What a scope does not name, it does not permit.

### C.1 `debugging_evaluation` — the default

Lets us debug the system, evaluate how agents perform, and count your trace in
aggregate figures. Does not permit training anything.

### C.2 `benchmark_only`

Adds permission to build benchmarks from the trace. Still no training.

### C.3 `ranking_training`

Everything above, plus training rankers — models that score or order results.
Not generative model training.

### C.4 `model_training`

Everything above, plus inclusion in training data for models. This is the
broadest trace-content scope, and the one to think hardest about, because a
trained model cannot be untrained — see D.3.

### C.5 `public_attribution` — not about your traces

Links your pseudonymous account to a public handle on the leaderboard and
profile pages. It grants no use of trace content whatsoever, and it is not
required to contribute. An enrolment holding only this scope cannot submit
traces at all.

> **Clause C.6.** The scopes above correspond exactly to the `ConsentScope`
> values recorded in the envelope. A use not permitted by a scope recorded in a
> given trace's envelope is not permitted for that trace, regardless of any
> scope granted later or by another contributor.

## D. Withdrawal and revocation

### D.1 How to take a trace back

Run `trace-commons-contributor daemon withdraw <submission-id>`, or
`--all-quarantined` for everything held in quarantine. Do it while you are still
logged in: withdrawal needs the account session, and logging out deletes it.

> **Clause D.1.** You may withdraw any submission at any time while your
> enrolment is active. Withdrawal revokes the licence granted in Clause A.5 for
> that trace, subject to Clause D.3.

### D.2 What that actually deletes

The encrypted artifact and every stored reference to it, including the copies
made at earlier processing stages. The deletion is checked: the system will not
report a successful withdrawal while content survives. Public figures that
included your trace are recomputed, and the site refuses to serve a snapshot
older than fifteen minutes — so a stale leaderboard breaks visibly instead of
quietly continuing to show you.

> **Clause D.2.** On withdrawal we delete the encrypted artifact, the file-side
> submission record, the stored object references, and artifacts held at
> status-derived storage paths from earlier processing stages. Errors in
> deletion propagate as failures rather than being reported as success.
> Community snapshots are invalidated and recomputed; the published surface
> refuses to serve a snapshot older than fifteen minutes.

### D.3 What withdrawal cannot undo

Two things, and we would rather say so plainly than let you discover it: a
statistic that has already been published stays published, and a model that has
already been trained stays trained. Withdrawal is not retroactive over derived
artifacts that no longer contain your trace but were computed with it.

> **Clause D.3.** Withdrawal does not require us to retract aggregate figures
> already published, nor to retrain, alter or destroy models or other derived
> artifacts produced before the withdrawal took effect. Aggregates recomputed
> after withdrawal will exclude the withdrawn trace.
